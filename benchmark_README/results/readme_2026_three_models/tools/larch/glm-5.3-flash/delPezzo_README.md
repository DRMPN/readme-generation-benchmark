## Overview

**delPezzo** is a research code for studying **(weak) del Pezzo surfaces** over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. It lets you:

- construct del Pezzo surfaces by **blowing up** ℙ² (including configurations of infinitely near points, i.e. the *weak* setting);
- compute the **Picard lattice**, the canonical class, and the **(-1)- and (-2)-curves** of a surface;
- compute the **effective cone** and the **nef cone** from the classes of negative curves;
- **contract** (-1)-curves and track the resulting **ADE singularities**;
- **search for cylinders** (open subsets isomorphic to 𝔸¹ × 𝔸¹) and output them in canonical form, up to the action of the Weyl/automorphism group;
- **cross-check** the computed outputs against classifications from the literature:
  - the degree-2 classification of cylinders of **Kim–Park**,
  - the family lists of **degenerate del Pezzo surfaces** published by **J. Lubbes** (snapshot included as `Lubbes_list.txt`).

The guiding philosophy is that classifications stated case-by-case in papers should be *reproducible*: every mathematical claim in the literature that is encoded here appears as a `pytest` test that re-derives it from scratch.

## Mathematical Background

### del Pezzo surfaces

A smooth projective surface $X$ over an algebraically closed field (here: characteristic 0) is a **del Pezzo surface** if $-K_X$ is ample. The **degree** is $d = K_X^2 \in \{1, \dots, 9\}$.

For $d \le 8$, $X$ is the blow-up of ℙ² at $d$ points in *general position*; for $d = 8$ there is a second model, ℙ¹ × ℙ¹, and $d = 9$ is ℙ² itself.

The Picard group $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$, equipped with the intersection form, is the odd unimodular lattice $I_{1,d}$ with basis $H, E_1, \dots, E_d$ and

$$K_X = -3H + E_1 + \cdots + E_d .$$

The orthogonal complement $K_X^\perp$ is a **root lattice** whose type — and hence the Weyl group acting on the Picard lattice — depends on the degree:

| degree $d$ | root system in $K_X^\perp$ | # of (−1)-curves |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | $E_8$ | 240 |
| 2 | $E_7$ | 56 |
| 3 | $E_6$ | 27 |
| 4 | $D_5$ | 10 |
| 5 | $A_4$ | 5 |
| 6 | $A_2 \times A_1$ | 6 |
| 7 | $A_1$ | 3 |
| 8 | — | 1 ($F_1$) / 0 (ℙ¹×ℙ¹) |
| 9 | — | 0 |

The **cone of curves** of a del Pezzo surface is generated by the classes of its (−1)-curves; dually, the **nef cone** is cut out by the conditions $D \cdot C \ge 0$ for all such classes $C$. These facts are the computational backbone of `cone.py`.

### Weak del Pezzo surfaces and ADE singularities

A surface with $-K_X$ **nef and big** (but not ample) is a **weak del Pezzo surface**. Such a surface is the blow-up of ℙ² at points in *almost* general position and additionally contains **(−2)-curves**. Contracting the (−2)-curves produces **ADE singularities**, and the anticanonical morphism factors through this contraction: the image is a **degenerate del Pezzo surface** — a del Pezzo surface with ADE singularities and ample anticanonical divisor.

Conversely, every degenerate del Pezzo surface arises this way, so the classification of weak del Pezzo surfaces of a given degree is equivalent to the classification of the associated ADE singularity data. This is precisely the classification published online by **Johannes Lubbes** (degrees ≥ 2), a snapshot of which ships with this repository and is parsed by `Lubbes_parse.py`.

### Cylinders

A **cylinder** on a surface $X$ is an open subset $U \subseteq X$ isomorphic to 𝔸¹ × 𝔸¹ (or, more generally, admitting a structure of 𝔸¹-fibration over a curve). The **boundary** $D = X \setminus U$ is a union of curves; on a del Pezzo surface of **Picard number one**, the boundary of any cylinder is the support of an anticanonical divisor.

Classifying cylinders therefore reduces to a finite, purely lattice-theoretic problem:

1. enumerate divisor classes (and their effective decompositions) on the Picard lattice;
2. identify candidates whose boundary is a union of (−1)/(−2)-curves with the right intersection pattern;
3. verify the cylinder condition for the complement;
4. quotient the resulting list by the action of the Weyl group (automorphisms).

`finder.py` implements this pipeline; `cylinder.py` provides the model and the verification criteria; the `test_deg*.py` files compare the output against the published lists.

## Modules in Detail

### `surface.py`

The heart of the package. The `Surface` class stores:

- the **degree** $K_X^2$ and **Picard rank**;
- the **Picard lattice** with its intersection form and the canonical class;
- the list of **(−1)-curve classes** (anticanonical) and **(−2)-curve classes** (root classes, in the weak case);
- the **contraction data**: contracting (−2)-curves yields the ADE type of the anticanonical model.

Surfaces are created either directly by degree or by **blowing up** an existing surface at a point (given by a divisor class); contractions of (−1)-curves recover the surface of degree $d-1$.

### `cone.py`

Implements the **effective cone** and the **nef cone** at the level of divisor classes:

- generators of the effective cone = classes of negative curves;
- nef cone = dual cone, described by intersection inequalities;
- decomposition utilities: writing a divisor class as a sum of effective classes (needed for candidate anticanonical divisors in the cylinder search).

### `cylinder.py`

A `Cylinder` object represents an open subset $U = X \setminus D$ together with:

- the **boundary classes** and their intersection graph;
- the check that the complement satisfies the **cylinder criterion** (𝔸¹-fibration / product 𝔸¹ × 𝔸¹);
- a **canonical representative**, so that cylinders related by an automorphism of the surface are identified.

### `finder.py`

The search engine. It enumerates candidate boundaries — typically decompositions of the anticanonical class into effective curve classes — **up to the symmetry group** (Weyl group on the Picard lattice, permutations of geometric points), applies the cone and cylinder criteria from `cone.py` / `cylinder.py`, and returns the complete, canonical list of cylinders. Results are cached so repeated queries during testing are fast.

### `Lubbes_parse.py`

Reads `Lubbes_list.txt` and turns each entry into a structured record (degree, singularity type, family identifier). Used by `test_Lubbes.py` to check that the surfaces constructed by this package match Lubbes' published families entry-by-entry.

## Getting Started

### Requirements

- **Python ≥ 3.9**
- **SageMath ≥ 9.x** — the mathematical backend (lattices, polynomial arithmetic, blow-up machinery). Install e.g. via conda-forge.
- **pytest** — test runner (declared in `pyproject.toml`).
- *(optional)* **Jupyter** — to run `tests.ipynb`.

### Installation

```bash
# 1) Get a Sage environment (recommended: conda-forge)
conda create -n sage -c conda-forge sagemath
conda activate sage

# 2) Clone and install (editable)
git clone https://github.com/<user>/delPezzo-rjflgle1.git
cd delPezzo-rjflgle1
pip install -e .
```

Alternatively, install into Sage's own Python:

```bash
sage -pip install -e .
```

> If you prefer to work from a checkout without installing, running scripts from the repository root with plain imports (e.g. `import surface`) also works, since the sources live at the top level.

### Sanity check

```bash
python -c "import delPezzo; print(delPezzo.__doc__)"
```

## Usage

The snippets below are illustrative; consult the module docstrings for the exact signatures.

### 1. Creating surfaces

```python
from delPezzo.surface import Surface

X5 = Surface(5)               # smooth del Pezzo of degree 5 (P2 blown up at 5 points)
X  = Surface(2, weak=True)    # weak del Pezzo of degree 2 (may carry (-2)-curves)

X.degree          # 2
X.picard_rank()   # Picard number
X.K               # canonical class on the Picard lattice
```

### 2. Curves and cones

```python
X.minus_one_curves()   # 56 classes for a degree-2 surface
X.minus_two_curves()   # root classes: (-2)-curves, present only in the weak case

from delPezzo.cone import Cone
C = Cone(X)
C.effective_generators()   # classes generating the cone of curves
C.nef_generators()         # facets of the dual (nef) cone
```

### 3. Blow-ups and contractions

```python
Y = X5.blowup(p)      # degree drops: 5 -> 4
Z = Y.contract(curve) # contract a (-1)-curve: degree rises back, singularity bookkeeping updated
```

Contractions of (−2)-curves on a weak surface produce the **ADE type** of the degenerate del Pezzo model — exactly the data tabulated by Lubbes.

### 4. Searching for cylinders

```python
from delPezzo.finder import find_cylinders

cyls = find_cylinders(X)                  # all cylinders, canonical up to Aut(X)
for c in cyls:
    print(c.boundary_classes(), c.is_product())
```

The finder enumerates candidates on the Picard lattice, so the answer is **complete** and independent of any field-specific choices.

### 5. Lubbes' database

```python
from delPezzo.Lubbes_parse import load_families

families = load_families("Lubbes_list.txt")
# records: (degree, ADE singularity data, family id) — directly comparable
# with the surfaces constructed by this package.
```

### 6. Interactive tour

Open **`tests.ipynb`** in Jupyter for a step-by-step walkthrough: building surfaces degree by degree, listing their curves, and reproducing the cylinder tables of the cited papers.

## Test Suite

Run everything with:

```bash
pytest -v
```

Tests that need a Sage backend are skipped automatically if SageMath cannot be imported. Tips:

- select one degree: `pytest test_deg3.py -v`;
- long enumerations (e.g. degree 1, with its 240 (−1)-curves) are the slowest — use `-k` to narrow the selection.

| File | What it verifies |
|---|---|
| `test_surface.py` | Core invariants: degree, $K^2$, Picard rank, canonical class; consistency after blow-ups/contractions. |
| `test_blowups.py` | Blow-up construction; orbits of points/curve classes under the (Weyl/automorphism) group. |
| `test_contraction.py` | Contracting a (−1)-curve raises the degree by one; ADE data recorded on contraction of (−2)-curves. |
| `test_finder.py` | Completeness and canonicity of the cylinder search on small, independently checkable cases. |
| `test_deg1.py` | Degree 1: $E_8$ root system, 240 (−1)-curves; cylinder list. |
| `test_deg2.py` | Degree 2: general structure and cylinder checks. |
| `test_deg2_KimPark.py` | **Literature check**: re-derives the Kim–Park classification of cylinders on (weak) del Pezzo surfaces of degree 2. |
| `test_deg2_B2.py` | **Literature check**: the degree-2 case study labelled “B2” in the cited source (see `bibliography.rst`). |
| `test_deg3.py` | Degree 3: $E_6$, 27 (−1)-curves (lines on a cubic surface); cylinder list. |
| `test_deg4.py` | Degree 4: $D_5$, 10 (−1)-curves; cylinder list. |
| `test_deg5.py` | Degree 5: $A_4$, 5 (−1)-curves; cylinder list. |

## Data Files

### `Lubbes_list.txt`

A plain-text snapshot of **Johannes Lubbes' list of degenerate del Pezzo surfaces** (del Pezzo surfaces with ADE singularities of degree ≥ 2), organized by degree and singularity type. It serves as the ground-truth dataset for `test_Lubbes.py`. Re-download from the author's page if you need a newer version, and re-run the tests afterwards.

### `bibliography.rst`

The bibliography in **reStructuredText**. Each literature-dependent test cites its source here, so every encoded classification traces back to a published paper. The main sources are:

- **J. Kim, E. Park**, *Cylinders on weak del Pezzo surfaces of degree 2* — basis for `test_deg2_KimPark.py`.
- **I. Cheltsov, J. Park** and coauthors, *Cylinders in singular del Pezzo surfaces* — general theory of cylinders on del Pezzo surfaces with singularities.
- **J. Lubbes**, *Families of del Pezzo surfaces*; *Minimal families of del Pezzo surfaces* — the family lists underlying `Lubbes_list.txt`.
- **Yu. I. Manin**, *Cubic Forms*; **M. Demazure**, *Surfaces de Del Pezzo* — classical references for the Picard lattice and the Weyl-group action.
- **P. Hacking, Yu. Prokhorov**, *Special degenerations of del Pezzo surfaces* — background on degenerate del Pezzo surfaces.

## Design Notes

- **Everything happens on the Picard lattice.** Curves, cones, and cylinders are represented by divisor classes with the intersection form; the only place where geometry enters is the verification of the cylinder criterion. Consequently, all results are valid over any algebraically closed field (characteristic 0) admitting the relevant configuration.
- **Canonical output.** All enumerations quotient by the natural symmetry group (Weyl group / point permutations), so outputs are lists of *orbit representatives* — stable and directly comparable with the tables in the literature.
- **Literature as test oracle.** Published classifications are not hard-coded: they are *recomputed* and compared entry-by-entry. If the tests pass, the search engine agrees with the papers.
